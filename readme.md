# Beamforming for RIS-assisted Near-Field Communications with Broadband Beamforming [with code]

Introduction: The sixth-generation (6G) wireless network aims to further enhance high throughput, achieve massive interconnectivity, and improve energy efficiency. To realize these promising goals, ultra-large arrays of antennas (ELAAs) and extremely high frequencies form a promising technology solution pair. Specifically, as a new type of metamaterial antenna, the reconfigurable intelligent surface (RIS) technology has been considered one of the most promising candidate ELAA solutions for building an intelligent radio environment [1], [2]. In this case, due to the increase in the Rayleigh distance associated with the array aperture and communication frequency, the near-field boundary in 6G communication will significantly expand [3]. Considering the abundant available bandwidth in high frequency, such as millimeter wave (mmWave) and terahertz (THz), near-field broadband RIS communication is becoming an emerging communication paradigm in the era of 6G [4].
In the near-field broadband RIS system, compared to the classical far-field narrow-band system, new electromagnetic (EM) characteristics need to be considered. Firstly, unlike the plane wavefront assumption in far-field channel modeling, due to the spherical wavefront in near-field radiation, the near-field channel involves angle and distance dimensions, leading to near-field beam focusing effect instead of far-field beam steering [5], [6]. Secondly, due to the large bandwidth between different subcarriers, the near-field broadband channel can be strongly frequency-dependent. However, for the popular hybrid beamforming architecture in ELAA systems, typical analog beamformers are frequency-independent. Therefore, the beams generated at different frequencies can focus at different positions, which is known as beam splitting effect [7]. Especially, in RIS-supported broadband communication, since the reflective units at RIS only perform passive phase shift operations, the illuminated and reflected beams at RIS will also be divided into different physical directions at different frequencies. In this case, the specific attributes of the frequency-independent analog precoding at the base station (BS) and the phase shift at the RIS cause unique dual beam splitting effects [8]. Therefore, the research on effective frequency-dependent beam forming architecture and optimization schemes urgently needs the near-field broadband RIS system.
In this context, this paper investigates the near-field wideband beamforming design for a Multiple Input Multiple Output (MIMO)-Orthogonal Frequency Division Multiplexing (OFDM) system with RIS assistance. The main contributions are summarized as follows:
1. To study the impact of dual beam splitting effects in near-field wideband RIS systems on beamforming performance, we investigated a frequency-dependent hybrid precoding and phase shifting structure. Specifically, by introducing sub-connectivity TDD units, a frequency-dependent hybrid precoding architecture is equipped for base stations to handle the near-field beam splitting effects at the base station. Building on this, considering the wideband beam splitting effect of RIS, two RIS structures based on true time delay (TTD-RIS) and virtual subarray (SA-RIS) were proposed to realize frequency-dependent passive beamforming for RIS.
2. We propose an end-to-end (E2 E) beamforming optimization framework based on deep learning to maximize the effective spectral efficiency of RIS-assisted MIMO-OFDM systems. This model consists of an uplink channel training (UL-CT) module and a downlink beamforming (DL-BF) module. By designing learnable combiner matrices at the base station and phase shifts at the relay system, joint optimization of beamforming and channel estimation is realized under limited pilot overhead. Unlike traditional channel estimators that use predefined combination matrices and reflection patterns, the combiner matrices and phase shifts in the UL-CT module can be adaptively adjusted according to dynamic wireless environments.
· On the basis of the proposed E2E optimization framework, combined with advanced neural network structures and classic communication signal processing methods, we have developed an efficient signal-guided beamforming network structure. Specifically, in the ULCT module, we designed an polarization-aware structure to simulate typical communication signals' filtering in both frequency and spatiotemporal domains, learning fine-grained effective latent channel semantic information from received pilots. To address the sparsity of channels in high-frequency ELAA systems, we propose a learnable ELAA algorithm based on Discrete Fourier Transform (DFT), which utilizes DFT to introduce the DL-BF module for guiding and accelerating the convergence of the beamforming network.
· Our numerical results demonstrate that the superior performance of our proposed E2E model can achieve better than traditional beamforming benchmarks. Specifically, compared to traditional hybrid precoding and classical RIS architectures, the proposed TTD-RIS and SA-RIS can effectively mitigate near-field dual-beam splitting effects. Moreover, the proposed E2E model allows for joint optimization of active and passive beamforming using implicit CSI, reducing the required training overhead and enhancing effective spectral efficiency. Additionally, the robustness and generalization of the proposed E2E model are evaluated under various system settings.
The conclusion proposes a near-field wideband beamforming scheme based on deep learning, addressing the performance loss in beamforming due to the near-field dual-beam splitting effect in MIMO systems. Initially, two specific RIS architectures were introduced: frequency-dependent passive beamforming using TTD-RIS and SA-RIS. Compared to the SA-RIS architecture, the TTD-RIS architecture achieves superior uplink beamforming performance but requires more energy consumption and hardware costs due to the introduction of TTD units. Furthermore, an E2E (End-to-End) beamforming optimization framework was proposed for joint design of high-dimensional channel estimation and frequency-related wideband beamforming. Additionally, to accelerate the convergence of the proposed E2E model, a highly efficient beamforming network backbone was developed by integrating advanced deep learning architectures with classical communication signal processing theory. Numerical results demonstrate that the proposed E2E model outperforms existing benchmarks for wideband beamforming without explicit CSI (Channel State Information), exhibiting superior beamforming performance and robustness.
